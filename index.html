▶ Why L5 is the hardest layer to break

After tokens are built and joined, L5 lifts every letter out of its token, rearranges them all in a ceil(√L) × rows grid read by column, then puts them back. The ~~ separators and @ tag markers are frozen in place while all letters around them shift — so in the ciphertext, the 2 letters after every @ are not the real tag letters, and the letters inside each apparent token span come from multiple different original tokens.

▶ Why the tag is hard to find

The length tag is encoded as two letters using a fixed scrambled formula (n×7+5 mod 26), not as a plain number. Combined with L5 scattering those letters to random positions, an attacker can't determine word lengths even if they know the tag format.

▶ Layer stack

Encode: L1 → L2 → L3 → L4, then assemble, then L5
Decode: un-L5, split, then un-L4 → L3 → un-L2 → un-L1 per token
L3 is self-inverse. L5 is undone by reversing the column-read permutation.
